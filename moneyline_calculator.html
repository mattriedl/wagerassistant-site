Understanding Moneyline Odds

Negative moneyline (e.g. −150): This is the favorite. You must bet $150 to win $100 in profit. The lower (more negative), the heavier the favorite.

Positive moneyline (e.g. +200): This is the underdog. A $100 bet wins $200 in profit. The higher the number, the bigger the underdog.

Formulas:
Favorite: Profit = Stake × (100 / |Moneyline|)
Underdog: Profit = Stake × (Moneyline / 100)
Implied Prob (Favorite): |ML| / (|ML| + 100)
Implied Prob (Underdog): 100 / (ML + 100)

The Moneyline Payout Formula Explained

A moneyline bet is simply a wager on who wins, priced in American odds. This moneyline calculator converts positive and negative prices into exact payouts so you can see profit and total return before you bet.

Formula & Worked Example

For positive odds, profit is Stake x (odds / 100); for negative odds it is Stake x (100 / |odds|). A $100 bet at +150 wins 100 x (150/100) = $150 profit ($250 back). The same $100 at -150 wins 100 x (100/150) = $66.67 profit ($166.67 back). Favorites risk more to win less; underdogs risk less to win more.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Reading +150 and -150 as the same payout - they are very different.
  • Forgetting favorites require a larger stake to earn a given profit.
  • Confusing total return with profit.
  • Ignoring the vig between the two sides when line shopping.
