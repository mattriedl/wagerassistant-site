How Parlays Work

A parlay combines multiple selections into one bet. All legs must win for the bet to pay out. Combined Decimal Odds = Leg 1 × Leg 2 × … × Leg N. Payout = Stake × Combined Odds. The more legs, the higher the potential payout — and the lower the probability of winning.

Our parlay calculator combines multiple bets into one ticket and shows the combined odds, total payout and profit instantly. Add as many legs as you like in American, decimal or fractional odds and this free multi-leg parlay calculator multiplies them together so you can see the explosive payouts parlays are famous for.

Parlays offer huge upside because every leg must win, multiplying the odds together. Whether you are building a 2-team teaser-style ticket or a long-shot 10-leg parlay, this parlay payout calculator shows the true risk and reward before you bet.

Pro Tips for Smarter Betting

  • Each added leg multiplies the odds but also multiplies the risk — more legs means a much lower win probability.
  • Compare a parlay's combined implied probability against your own estimate to judge value.
  • Consider round robins to cover smaller combinations instead of one all-or-nothing ticket.

The Parlay Odds Formula Explained

A parlay multiplies the decimal odds of every leg together, then multiplies that product by your stake. Because the odds compound, even modest prices snowball into large payouts - and the combined win probability shrinks just as fast.

Formula & Worked Example

The formula is Combined Odds = Leg1 x Leg2 x ... x LegN, then Payout = Stake x Combined Odds. Take a 3-leg parlay at -110 (1.9091), +150 (2.50) and -200 (1.50). Multiplying gives 1.9091 x 2.50 x 1.50 = 7.159. A $20 stake returns 20 x 7.159 = $143.18, a profit of $123.18. The combined implied probability is just 1 / 7.159 = 13.97%, which is why parlays are high-risk, high-reward.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Adding a low-value leg just to boost the payout, which usually lowers overall expected value.
  • Assuming more legs is always better - each leg multiplies risk, not just reward.
  • Forgetting that one losing leg voids the entire parlay.
  • Comparing combined odds between books without checking each leg's price.
