Understanding Implied Probability

Implied probability is the percentage chance of an outcome happening, as reflected by the odds offered. It's the key to identifying value bets.

Conversion Formulas:

  • Decimal → Probability: 1 / decimal × 100
  • American (-): odds / (odds + 100) × 100
  • American (+): 100 / (odds + 100) × 100
  • Probability → Decimal: 100 / probability

Bookmaker Margin: The total implied probability across all outcomes exceeds 100%. The excess is the bookmaker's built-in profit margin (vig/juice). A market at 104% has a ~4% margin. Lower margins mean fairer odds for bettors.

Fair Probability: Divide each implied probability by the total to remove the margin and get true probabilities that sum to 100%.

Why Use Our Implied Probability Calculator?

Implied probability translates abstract odds into an intuitive percentage you can reason about. If you believe the true chance is higher than the implied probability, the bet has value — making this the simplest, most powerful tool for value betting.

Pro Tips for Smarter Betting

  • Implied probability = 1 ÷ decimal odds × 100%.
  • Add up both sides of a market to see the bookmaker's overround.
  • Bet only when your estimated probability comfortably exceeds the implied figure.

The Implied Probability Formula Explained

Implied probability is the win chance baked into a set of odds. This implied probability calculator converts any American, decimal or fractional price into a percentage so you can compare it with your own estimate and spot value.

Formula & Worked Example

From decimal odds the formula is Implied Probability = (1 / decimal odds) x 100. So +200 (decimal 3.00) implies 1/3.00 = 33.3%, while -150 (decimal 1.667) implies 1/1.667 = 60%. If you believe the true chance is higher than the implied figure, the bet has value; if lower, it does not.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Forgetting the vig inflates every implied probability above the true chance.
  • Assuming implied probability is the book's honest prediction.
  • Not summing a full market, which reveals the overround.
  • Comparing implied probabilities from different-margin books directly.
