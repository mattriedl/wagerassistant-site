How Dutching Works

Dutching distributes your total stake proportionally across multiple selections so that the return is equal regardless of which selection wins. The stake for each selection is: Stake_i = Total Stake × (1/Odds_i) / Σ(1/Odds_j). Dutching is profitable when the combined implied probability of your selections is less than 100%.

The dutching calculator splits a total stake across several selections so you win the same amount no matter which one comes in. Enter each selection's odds and your total stake, and this dutch betting calculator returns the precise stake for each runner along with your guaranteed profit and ROI.

Why Use Our Dutching Calculator?

Backing several selections individually rarely produces an even return. Dutching balances your stakes mathematically so every winning outcome pays the same, turning a multi-runner opinion into a single, predictable position.

Pro Tips for Smarter Betting

  • Dutching works best when you can confidently eliminate most of the field.
  • Compare your combined stake's implied probability with the true chance to ensure value.
  • Use it across bookmakers to combine the best price on each selection.

The Dutching Formula Explained

Dutching splits your stake across several selections so you win the same amount regardless of which one comes in. This dutching calculator sizes each bet in proportion to its odds to equalize the return.

Formula & Worked Example

Each stake is (Total Stake x (1/odds)) / (sum of 1/odds). Dutching three horses at 3.0, 4.0 and 5.0 with a $100 total: the implied probabilities are 0.3333, 0.25 and 0.20, summing to 0.7833. That sub-1.0 total means a guaranteed profit - each selection returns 100 / 0.7833 = $127.66, a $27.66 profit whichever of the three wins.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Dutching a market where the implied probabilities sum above 1.0, which locks in a loss.
  • Rounding stakes so the returns are no longer equal.
  • Forgetting that you still lose everything if none of your selections win.
  • Including too many runners, which pushes the total probability over 100%.

When the probabilities sum below 1.0 across books, it becomes an arb - check with the arbitrage betting calculator. Equalize two-way positions with the hedging calculator. See our sports betting tips for beginners.
