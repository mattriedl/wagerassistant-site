Pro Tips for Smarter Betting

  • Progressions escalate fast — always check the maximum exposure first.
  • Never use recovery staking to chase losses beyond your bankroll limits.
  • Flat or Kelly staking is generally safer for long-term bankroll health.

How Staking Progressions Work

A betting progression adjusts your stake after each result to chase a target profit or recover losses. This bet progression calculator maps out each step so you can see exactly how fast the required stake - and your exposure - grows.

Formula & Worked Example

In a simple loss-recovery progression at even money, each stake covers all prior losses plus the base profit. Starting at $10, a losing run forces stakes of $10, $20, $40, $80, $160 - and after five losses you have risked $310 to win just $10. The formula for step n in a doubling progression is Stake(n) = Base x 2^(n-1), which is why bankrolls and table limits get hit fast.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Believing a progression changes the underlying odds - it never does.
  • Underestimating how quickly stakes escalate after a losing streak.
  • Hitting the sportsbook's maximum bet mid-progression, breaking the plan.
  • Risking a huge bankroll to grind out a tiny fixed profit.

Frequently Asked Questions

A betting progression is a staking plan where the size of each bet is adjusted — usually increased after losses — to recover prior losses and lock in a target profit when a bet finally wins.
Progressions do not change the underlying edge of your bets. They can smooth short-term results but risk large losses during long losing runs, so use them cautiously.
Each stake must cover accumulated losses plus the target profit at the given odds. Our progression calculator computes the required stake for each step automatically.
Maximum exposure is the total amount you would lose if every bet in the progression lost. The calculator highlights this so you can manage risk.
