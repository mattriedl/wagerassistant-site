How Round Robins Work

A round robin bet creates every possible parlay combination of a chosen size from your selections. For example, 4 selections with 2-team parlays = 6 individual bets. Each parlay is a separate wager at your chosen stake. If all selections win, every parlay pays out. If one loses, only parlays containing that selection lose — the rest still pay.

The round robin calculator builds every smaller parlay combination from your list of selections, spreading risk compared with one all-or-nothing ticket. Enter your selections and choose the parlay sizes, and this round robin bet calculator shows the number of bets, total stake and potential payout.

A round robin lets you keep winning even if one selection lets you down, because your picks are combined into multiple overlapping parlays. Our round robin parlay calculator handles the combinatorics for you.

How Round Robin Bets Work

A round robin breaks a group of selections into every smaller parlay combination. This round robin calculator builds all the 2-leg, 3-leg and larger parlays automatically so one losing pick does not wipe out your entire ticket.

Formula & Worked Example

With 3 selections you get 3 two-leg parlays (A+B, A+C, B+C). At $10 per combo that is a $30 outlay. If each leg is -110 (1.9091), each winning double returns 10 x 1.9091 x 1.9091 = $36.44. Win all three picks and you collect 3 x $36.44 = $109.32; win only two of three and you still cash one double for $36.44, softening the loss a straight parlay would have made total.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Underestimating total cost - combinations multiply quickly with more picks.
  • Expecting full-parlay payouts; round robins spread stake across many smaller bets.
  • Confusing the number of selections with the number of bets placed.
  • Choosing too many picks and diluting the return per winning combo.
