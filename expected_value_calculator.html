Understanding Expected Value

EV = (Win Probability × Profit if Win) − (Loss Probability × Stake)
A positive EV bet means you expect to profit long-term. A negative EV bet favors the bookmaker. The break-even probability is the minimum win rate needed to be profitable at these odds (= 1 / Decimal Odds).

The expected value calculator reveals whether a bet is profitable in the long run. Enter the odds, your stake and your estimated win probability, and this EV betting calculator returns the expected value per bet, your expected ROI and the break-even probability the price requires.

The Expected Value Formula Explained

Expected value (EV) is the average profit or loss a bet would produce if you could place it infinitely many times. This expected value calculator weighs your win probability against the payout to tell you whether a wager is +EV (worth making) or -EV.

Formula & Worked Example

The formula is EV = (P(win) x Profit) - (P(lose) x Stake). Bet $100 at +120 (profit $120) with a 50% win chance: EV = (0.50 x 120) - (0.50 x 100) = 60 - 50 = +$10. That is a +10% EV bet - over the long run it earns $10 per $100 staked. Flip the probability to 45% and EV turns negative at -$1.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using the book's implied probability as your own - that guarantees zero edge.
  • Treating a single winning bet as proof of +EV; EV only matters over the long run.
  • Forgetting to subtract the stake when the bet loses.
  • Ignoring the vig baked into the quoted odds.

Once a bet is +EV, size it with the kelly criterion calculator and remove the vig using the no vig calculator. Our bankroll management strategies guide explains why +EV plus discipline wins.
