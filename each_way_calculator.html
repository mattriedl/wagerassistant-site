How Each-Way Bets Work

An each-way bet is actually two separate bets — a win bet and a place bet — so it costs double your unit stake. The win part pays at full odds if your selection wins. The place part pays at a fraction of the odds (typically 1/4 or 1/5) if your selection finishes in one of the paid places.

Example: A $10 each-way bet at 10/1 with 1/4 odds and 3 places costs $20 total ($10 win + $10 place). If the horse wins, you get $110 (win) + $35 (place) = $145 return. If it places 2nd or 3rd, you get only the $35 place return.

Each-Way Terms:

  • 1/4 odds: Most common for horse racing (5+ runners)
  • 1/5 odds: Standard for handicaps with 16+ runners
  • 1/3 odds: Common in golf outright markets

Rule 4: When a horse is withdrawn, a deduction is applied to winning and place bets to compensate for the removed runner's odds.

How Each-Way Bets Work

An each-way bet is really two bets: one on your selection to win and one on it to place. This each way calculator settles both parts at the correct place terms so you see the full return whether your pick wins or just places.

Formula & Worked Example

You stake twice - e.g. $10 each way is $20 total. Place terms are often 1/5 the odds for the top few finishers. On an 11/1 (12.00) selection: if it wins, the win part returns $120 and the place part pays at 1/5 of 11/1 (so 2.2/1, decimal 3.20) returning $32 - a total of $152. If it only places, you collect the $32 place return and lose the $10 win stake.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Forgetting an each-way bet costs double your unit stake.
  • Misreading the place terms (fraction and number of places).
  • Backing short-priced favorites each-way, where place returns barely beat the stake.
  • Assuming place terms are the same at every book - they vary by race.
