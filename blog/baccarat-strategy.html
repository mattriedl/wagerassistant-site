Baccarat Strategy: The Smart Bets and the Math Behind Them

Key takeaways

  • The banker bet has the lowest house edge at about 1.06%.
  • The player bet is close behind at 1.24% — both are solid.
  • Avoid the tie bet; its house edge is around 14%.
  • Baccarat is a game of chance — systems cannot beat the edge.

Baccarat has a reputation for glamour and complexity, but it is actually one of the simplest casino games to play and among the best for the player. There are only three bets, no decisions once wagers are placed, and the two main bets have very low house edges. The entire strategy comes down to which bet you make.

How baccarat works

In baccarat, two hands are dealt — the 'Player' and the 'Banker' — and you bet on which will total closest to nine. Card values are simple: aces count as one, face cards and tens count as zero, and only the last digit of a total matters (a 7 and 8 sum to 15, which is 5). Third-card rules are fixed and handled automatically by the dealer, so you make no decisions after betting.

Note that 'Player' and 'Banker' are just names for the two hands — you can bet on either regardless of where you sit. This is what makes baccarat pure: your only real choice is which hand to back.

The three bets and their edges

  • Banker bet: house edge about 1.06% (after the standard 5% commission on wins).
  • Player bet: house edge about 1.24%.
  • Tie bet: house edge around 14.4% (sometimes higher depending on payout).

Those first two numbers are excellent — comparable to blackjack and far better than most casino games. The tie bet, despite its tempting 8:1 payout, is a trap.

Why banker is best

The banker hand wins slightly more than half of all non-tie hands because of the asymmetric third-card drawing rules that favor it. Casinos offset this by charging a 5% commission on banker wins — yet even after that commission, the banker remains the lowest-edge bet on the table at roughly 1.06%.

The practical takeaway is clear: when in doubt, bet banker. Over a long session, consistently backing the banker gives you the best mathematical position baccarat offers. The small commission is already baked into that 1.06% figure.

The tie trap

The tie bet pays 8:1 (occasionally 9:1), which lures players hoping for a big score. But ties are rare, and the payout does not come close to compensating for their infrequency. The result is a house edge around 14% — more than ten times worse than the banker bet. Avoid it entirely, along with most side bets, which carry similarly poor odds.

Bankroll and session tips

  1. Stick to the banker bet as your default; occasionally back the player for variety.
  2. Never make the tie bet or exotic side bets.
  3. Account for the 5% banker commission when tracking results.
  4. Set a loss limit and a win goal before you sit down.
  5. Remember every hand is independent — no 'trend' sheet predicts the next result.

No betting system or trend-tracking scorecard can beat baccarat's house edge, because each hand is statistically independent. The only strategy that matters is bet selection: favor the banker, respect your bankroll, and treat baccarat as the elegant, low-edge entertainment it is.
