Roulette Tips and Odds for Every Bet Type

Key takeaways

  • The house edge is 2.7% on European wheels but 5.26% on American.
  • No bet type on the wheel beats the house edge — payouts match the risk.
  • Betting systems like Martingale cannot overcome the math.
  • Choose European/French wheels and treat roulette as entertainment.

Roulette is a pure game of chance with no skill element, which makes understanding its odds especially important. Knowing the house edge of each bet and the difference between wheel types lets you play longer, lose slower, and avoid the myths that cost players money.

European vs American wheels

The most consequential decision in roulette is which wheel you play. A European wheel has 37 pockets (0–36) and a house edge of 2.70%. An American wheel adds a double-zero (00) for 38 pockets, nearly doubling the house edge to 5.26%.

That difference is enormous over time: the American wheel takes roughly twice as much of your money for the same play. Always choose a European or French wheel when available. French wheels often add the 'La Partage' rule, which returns half your even-money bet when the ball lands on zero, cutting the edge on those bets to just 1.35%.

Inside bets

Inside bets are placed on specific numbers and carry higher payouts with lower win probability:

  • Straight up (single number): pays 35:1, roughly a 2.7% chance on a European wheel.
  • Split (two numbers): pays 17:1.
  • Street (three numbers): pays 11:1.
  • Corner (four numbers): pays 8:1.
  • Line (six numbers): pays 5:1.

Crucially, every one of these payouts is calibrated so the house keeps the same 2.7% edge. No inside bet is 'better' than another in terms of expected value — they simply trade win frequency for payout size.

Outside bets

Outside bets cover large groups of numbers and pay less but hit more often, making them popular for players who want longer sessions:

  • Red/Black, Odd/Even, High/Low: pay 1:1, close to a 48.6% chance on a European wheel.
  • Dozens and Columns: pay 2:1, covering 12 numbers each.

These even-money bets feel safer and extend your playing time, but the house edge is identical to inside bets. They are a matter of style, not strategy.

The truth about systems

Betting systems like the Martingale (double your bet after every loss) promise to beat roulette, but they cannot change the underlying math. The Martingale fails because losing streaks happen more often than intuition suggests, table limits cap how far you can double, and a long run of losses leads to catastrophic bets chasing a tiny profit.

Every spin is independent — the wheel has no memory. Past results, 'hot' numbers, and 'due' colors are illusions. No pattern of betting can turn a negative-expectation game into a positive one.

Smart roulette tips

  1. Always play European or French wheels to halve the house edge.
  2. Look for French tables with La Partage or En Prison rules on even-money bets.
  3. Set a session budget and treat losses as the cost of entertainment.
  4. Ignore betting systems — they do not beat the house edge.
  5. Prefer even-money bets if your goal is longer play time, not bigger swings.

Roulette is best enjoyed as entertainment rather than a profit opportunity. Understanding the odds will not make you a long-term winner, but it will help you choose the lowest-edge options and avoid the costly myths that trap uninformed players.
