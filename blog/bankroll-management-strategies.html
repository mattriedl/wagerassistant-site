Bankroll Management Strategies That Keep You in the Game

Key takeaways

  • Bankroll management is what keeps you betting through inevitable losing streaks.
  • A 'unit' is typically 1% of your bankroll — bet in units, not dollars.
  • Flat staking is simplest; Kelly maximizes growth but is more aggressive.
  • Never increase stakes to chase losses — adjust only as your bankroll changes.

You can pick winners at a profitable rate and still go broke if you stake poorly. Bankroll management is the discipline that keeps you in the game long enough for your edge to pay off. It is arguably more important than handicapping itself, because even the sharpest bettor faces long losing runs that reckless staking turns fatal.

Why bankroll management matters

Betting results are volatile. Even a bettor winning 55% of even-money bets will experience losing streaks of six, eight, or more in a row over a season. Without a staking plan, a few bad runs — or one tilted, oversized bet — can wipe out months of careful work. A good plan smooths out variance and removes emotion from the size of each wager.

What is a unit?

Professionals talk in units rather than dollars. A unit is a fixed percentage of your bankroll, usually 1%. With a $2,000 bankroll, one unit is $20. Thinking in units standardizes your staking regardless of bankroll size and makes it easy to compare performance over time. A 'three-unit play' always means 3% of your current bankroll.

Flat staking

Flat staking means risking the same amount — typically 1–2 units — on every bet, regardless of how confident you feel. It is the simplest and most robust approach for beginners and intermediate bettors because it caps your downside and prevents emotional over-betting.

The main drawback is that it does not scale with edge: a bet you love gets the same stake as one you merely like. But its simplicity and protection against ruin make it the default recommendation for most recreational bettors.

Percentage staking

With percentage staking, you bet a fixed percentage of your current bankroll each time. As your bankroll grows, your bets grow; as it shrinks, your bets shrink automatically. This built-in protection makes it mathematically impossible to go fully broke from a losing run and lets winnings compound during hot stretches.

The trade-off is slower recovery after losses, since each losing bet reduces the next stake. Many bettors use a hybrid: percentage staking recalculated weekly rather than after every single bet.

The Kelly Criterion

The Kelly Criterion is a formula that calculates the mathematically optimal stake to maximize long-term bankroll growth, based on your edge and the odds. The formula is: stake fraction = (bp − q) ÷ b, where b is the decimal odds minus one, p is your probability of winning, and q is the probability of losing.

Kelly maximizes growth but can be aggressive and swingy. Because our probability estimates are never perfect, most practitioners use 'fractional Kelly' — betting a half or quarter of the recommended amount to reduce volatility. Rules to live by

  1. Only bet with money set aside for betting — never bills or savings.
  2. Define one unit as roughly 1% of your bankroll and bet in units.
  3. Never raise your stake purely to recover a loss (no chasing).
  4. Re-size your unit as your bankroll grows or shrinks, not game to game.
  5. Track results with the ROI Calculator to confirm your edge is real.

No staking plan turns a losing bettor into a winner — but the right plan ensures that if you do have an edge, you will still be around to profit from it. Pick an approach that matches your risk tolerance and follow it with discipline.
