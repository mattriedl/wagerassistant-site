Blackjack Basic Strategy: Tips to Shrink the House Edge

Key takeaways

  • Basic strategy is the mathematically correct play for every hand.
  • Perfect basic strategy cuts the house edge to roughly 0.5%.
  • Always split aces and eights; never split tens or fives.
  • Avoid insurance and side bets — they carry a high house edge.

Blackjack is one of the few casino games where skill genuinely matters. Using basic strategy — the mathematically optimal decision for every combination of your cards and the dealer's upcard — reduces the house edge from several percent down to around 0.5%. That makes blackjack one of the best bets on the casino floor, provided you play correctly.

How basic strategy works

Basic strategy was derived through computer simulation of millions of hands. For every possible player hand against every dealer upcard, there is one decision that maximizes your expected return over the long run. Memorizing these decisions does not guarantee you win any single hand, but it ensures you never give up value through a wrong play.

The dealer's upcard is the key input. When the dealer shows a weak card (2 through 6), they are more likely to bust, so you play more conservatively. When they show a strong card (7 through Ace), you must be more aggressive to compete.

Hard totals

A hard total is any hand without an ace, or where the ace must count as 1. Core rules:

  • Always hit a hard 8 or lower.
  • Hit 9 against a dealer 2 or 7+; double against 3–6.
  • Double hard 10 and 11 when your total beats the dealer's upcard.
  • Stand on hard 17 or higher, always.
  • With 12–16, stand against a weak dealer card (2–6) and hit against a strong one (7–Ace).

Soft totals

A soft total contains an ace counted as 11, meaning you cannot bust with one more card. This flexibility lets you play aggressively:

  • Always hit soft 17 (A-6) or lower unless doubling.
  • Double soft 13–18 against a dealer 5 or 6 when allowed.
  • Stand on soft 19 and 20.
  • Soft 18 (A-7) is nuanced: stand vs 2, 7, 8; double vs 3–6; hit vs 9, 10, Ace.

Pairs and splitting

When you are dealt two cards of the same rank you may split them into two hands. The most important rules here are among the easiest to remember:

  • Always split aces and eights. Two aces give you two chances at 21; splitting eights escapes a terrible 16.
  • Never split tens or fives. Two tens is a powerful 20 — keep it. Two fives is a 10, better played as a double.
  • Split 2s, 3s, 6s, 7s and 9s situationally, based on the dealer's upcard.

Rules that matter

Not all blackjack tables are equal. Before sitting down, check the rules, because they change the house edge significantly:

  • Seek tables that pay 3:2 for blackjack and avoid 6:5 games, which quietly increase the house edge.
  • Dealer standing on soft 17 is better for you than dealer hitting soft 17.
  • Fewer decks favor the player, all else equal.
  • The option to double after a split is a small bonus to look for.

Habits to avoid

Even players who know basic strategy leak money through avoidable mistakes. Skip the insurance bet — it is a side wager with a high house edge, not protection. Ignore flashy side bets for the same reason. And never make decisions based on hunches or what other players at the table are doing; the math does not care about streaks.

Blackjack rewards discipline. Combine solid basic strategy with sensible bankroll management and you will enjoy one of the lowest house edges in the casino. For sports bettors, the same principle applies: let the math, not emotion, drive every decision.
