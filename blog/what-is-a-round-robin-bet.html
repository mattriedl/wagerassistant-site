What Is a Round Robin Bet? How It Works, Payouts and Examples

Key takeaways

  • A round robin bet splits one list of picks into every smaller parlay of the size you choose (by 2s, by 3s and so on).
  • Total cost = stake per bet × number of parlays: 4 picks by 2s is 6 bets, and 8 picks at every size is 247 bets.
  • You can profit with a losing pick, but a perfect card pays far less than one big parlay for the same total stake.
  • Pushed or voided legs are usually dropped and each parlay is regraded with fewer legs; rules vary by sportsbook.
  • Round robins don't reduce the house margin: each parlay carries the stacked vig of all its legs.

A round robin bet is a set of smaller parlays built automatically from one list of picks, so you can still win money even if one or two of your selections lose. Instead of tying every pick into a single all-or-nothing parlay, the sportsbook pairs them into every possible 2-leg (or 3-leg, and so on) combination and charges you the same stake on each one.

That flexibility costs something: you pay for several bets instead of one, and a perfect ticket pays less than the same picks in one big parlay. This guide explains what a round robin bet means, how the combinations and payouts work with real numbers, how a push is usually settled, and when a round robin makes more sense than a straight parlay.

Quick answer: A round robin bet turns one list of picks into every possible smaller parlay of a size you choose. Three picks "by 2s" make three 2-leg parlays at the same stake each. You need at least two winners to cash anything, and your total cost is the stake per parlay multiplied by the number of parlays.

What is a round robin bet?

The name comes from round-robin tournaments, where every team plays every other team once. In betting, every pick on your slip gets "matched" with every other pick inside a series of parlays. FanDuel's betting guide puts it simply: a round robin is "a group of smaller Parlays created from the same set of picks" (FanDuel).

Here is the core round robin bet meaning in three points:

  • Selections: the individual picks you add to the slip, such as a point spread, a moneyline or a total. FanDuel, for example, asks for at least three.
  • Parlay size: how many legs each smaller parlay has. Books label this "by 2s", "by 3s" and so on.
  • Stake per bet: the amount you put on each smaller parlay. Your total stake is that amount times the number of parlays the slip creates.

Each smaller parlay is graded on its own. One losing pick kills every parlay it appears in, but the parlays that don't include it can still win. That is the whole appeal: a round robin parlay bet trades some upside for a softer landing when a pick misses. If you're new to parlays, start with our parlay betting tips first, then come back.

How does a round robin bet work?

Take three picks: Team A, Team B and Team C. A round robin by 2s builds every pair:

  1. Team A + Team B
  2. Team A + Team C
  3. Team B + Team C

That is three 2-leg parlays. At $10 per parlay, your total stake is $30. FanDuel's guide uses the same structure with three NFL spreads at $5 each, for $15 wagered across three parlays (FanDuel).

With four picks, a round robin by 2s builds six 2-leg parlays, and a round robin by 3s builds four 3-leg parlays. Many apps also let you tick more than one size at once, such as by 2s and by 3s together. The slip shows the total number of bets and the full cost before you confirm, so always read that line before you tap "place bet".

How much does a round robin bet pay?

Each winning parlay pays exactly what that parlay would pay on its own. You add up the winners, then subtract your total stake. Let's use four picks, each at -110 (1.91 in decimal odds), with $10 on each parlay.

A 2-leg parlay at -110/-110 has decimal odds of about 3.64 (roughly +264 in American odds), so a winning $10 parlay returns $36.45, which is $26.45 profit plus your $10 back. A 3-leg parlay at -110 on every leg returns $69.58 on a $10 stake.

Worked example: four picks by 2s ($60 total)

Picks that winWinning 2-leg parlaysTotal returnedProfit or loss
4 of 46$218.68+$158.68
3 of 43$109.34+$49.34
2 of 41$36.45-$23.55
1 or 0 of 40$0.00-$60.00

Compare that with putting the same $60 on one 4-leg parlay. If all four win, it returns $797.00 ($737.00 profit). If any one pick loses, you get nothing. The round robin by 2s gave up about $578 of upside on a perfect card in exchange for still showing a $49.34 profit at 3-of-4.

Same picks by 3s ($40 total)

By 3s creates four 3-leg parlays. Four winners return $278.32 (+$238.32 profit), three winners return $69.58 (+$29.58), and two or fewer lose the full $40. By 3s needs more of your picks to hit, but it pays more per winning ticket than by 2s.

Odds don't have to match. With three picks at -150, +120 and -110 by 2s ($10 each), the parlays pay $36.67, $31.82 and $42.00. All three winning returns $110.48 on $30 staked. If only the +120 pick loses, you keep a small $1.82 profit. Run mixed prices through the Parlay Calculator or convert them first with the Odds Converter.

Round robin vs parlay: what's the difference?

A round robin is not a different kind of wager so much as a bundle of parlays placed in one go. The differences that matter:

Single parlayRound robin
Number of bets1Several (3 to hundreds)
CostOne stakeStake per bet × number of bets
One pick losesWhole ticket losesOnly parlays with that pick lose
Payout if every pick winsHighestLower for the same total stake
Minimum winners to cashAll of themAs many as the parlay size (2 for by 2s)

Round robins are also different from same game parlays. Books generally don't let you round robin several selections from one game: FanDuel says a side, total and props from the same game "cannot be combined in a Round Robin" (FanDuel), and Fanatics Sportsbook's house rules say round robin wagers "cannot include selections from the same event" (Fanatics Sportsbook house rules). For correlated picks inside one game, see our same game parlay strategy guide.

What happens if a leg pushes or is voided?

A push happens when a bet lands exactly on the number, such as a team winning by 3 on a -3 spread. At most US sportsbooks, a pushed or voided leg is removed from each parlay it's in, and that parlay is regraded with one fewer leg. Fanatics Sportsbook's rules, for example, say the bet "will be settled using the remaining selections," becoming a smaller parlay "or a single wager in the event that only one leg remains" (Fanatics).

Example: three picks at -110 by 2s, $10 each. Pick C pushes, A and B win.

  • A + B stays a 2-leg parlay and returns $36.45.
  • A + C becomes a single bet on A and returns $19.09.
  • B + C becomes a single bet on B and returns $19.09.

You get back $74.63 on $30, a profit of $44.63. Settlement rules vary by sportsbook and state, especially for postponed games and ties in three-way markets, so check your book's house rules before you bet.

How to place a round robin bet

The steps are almost identical on every major US app, where the bet is legal:

  1. Add three or more selections from different games to your bet slip.
  2. Open the parlay or round robin section of the slip (some apps show it as "Round Robin" under the parlay option).
  3. Choose the parlay sizes you want, such as by 2s, by 3s or both.
  4. Enter your stake per bet, not the total.
  5. Check the number of bets and the total cost the slip displays.
  6. Confirm the wager.

You'll sometimes see a "2 bet round robin" or "2-pick round robin". Apps word this differently, but it usually means a round robin by 2s, where every parlay has two legs. The bet count on the slip tells you exactly what you're buying.

Is a round robin bet worth it?

A round robin doesn't change the math of each parlay, it only changes how many you buy. Every leg carries the sportsbook's margin, and parlays stack it. If each -110 pick is a true 50/50, the expected loss is about 4.5% of the stake on a single bet, 8.9% on a 2-leg parlay, 13.0% on a 3-leg parlay and 17.0% on a 4-leg parlay. A round robin by 2s at -110 therefore expects to lose about 8.9% of everything you stake, roughly double the cost of betting the same picks as singles. When a round robin can make sense

  • You want parlay-style payouts but don't want one miss to wipe out the whole ticket.
  • You're betting underdogs at plus odds, where 2-leg parlays pay well and one upset can cover the losers.
  • You believe your picks have real value at the posted prices. If they do, the parlay structure multiplies that edge; if they don't, it multiplies the house's. Our value betting strategy guide explains how to judge that.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Entering your total budget as the stake per bet. With 26 bets, $10 per bet is $260.
  • Selecting every parlay size on a long list of picks without checking the bet count.
  • Assuming you're "covered". With by 2s, two winners out of four still loses money in the example above.
  • Using round robins to chase losses. Keep the total stake within your normal unit size; our bankroll management guide shows how to set one.

Using a round robin bet calculator

Bottom line: a round robin is a way to spread risk across several parlays, not a way to beat the margin. Know your bet count, know your total stake, and treat it as a set of parlays priced the same way as any other.
