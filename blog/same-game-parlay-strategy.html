Understanding Same Game Parlays (SGPs)

A standard parlay involves picking winners or totals from different games. A Same Game Parlay allows you to stack multiple outcomes from the same game into one ticket.

For example, in an NFL matchup between the Kansas City Chiefs and the Las Vegas Raiders, a typical SGP might look like this:

  1. Chiefs -7.5 (Spread)
  2. Patrick Mahomes Over 285.5 Passing Yards
  3. Travis Kelce Anytime Touchdown Scorer

If all three events happen, you win. If even one fails, the entire bet is a loss. Because these events are related, sportsbooks don't simply multiply the odds like they do in a standard parlay; they use "correlated pricing" to adjust the payout.

Why Sportsbooks Love SGPs

Standard parlays have a house edge (or "vig") of around 10-15%. SGPs often have a hold of 20% to 30%. This is because many bettors pick outcomes that contradict each other or fail to account for how much the sportsbook is "shaving" the odds on correlated plays. Understanding implied probability is essential to seeing exactly how much value you are giving up.

The Core Principle: Correlative Betting

The most effective Same Game Parlay strategy is finding positive correlation. This means picking two or more events where if Event A happens, Event B is much more likely to happen.

Positive Correlation Examples

  • NFL: Betting a team to win (Moneyline) and their starting quarterback to go "Over" on passing yards. If the team is winning, the QB likely had a productive day.
  • NBA: Betting the "Over" on the total game points and the "Over" on a star player's points. High-scoring games usually feature big nights from the primary scorers.
  • MLB: Betting a team to win and their starting pitcher to record "Over" 5.5 strikeouts. Dominant pitching often leads to victories.

Negative Correlation (The SGP Killer)

Conversely, you must avoid negative correlation, where outcomes work against each other.

  • Avoid: Betting a team to win but their lead running back to go "Under" on rushing yards. While possible, it’s a counter-intuitive combination that decreases your mathematical chances.

NFL Same Game Parlay Tips

The NFL is the king of SGP betting. Here are three specific strategies for football:

1. The Game Script Strategy

Before placing a bet, visualize how the game will play out. This is called a "game script."

  • The Blowout Script: If you think the favorites will win by 20 points, bet the Favorite Spread, the Under on the underdog's rushing yards, and an Anytime TD for the favorite’s defense or special teams.
  • The Shootout Script: If you expect a high-scoring game with little defense, stack the Game Over, the Over on both quarterbacks' passing yards, and the Over on total field goals made.

2. Market Mispricing on Player Props

Sportsbooks are often slower to adjust player prop lines than they are to adjust game spreads. Look for injuries to secondary players that might force a star player into a higher usage rate. Use our Expected Value Calculator to see if the prop odds offer a "plus-EV" opportunity compared to historical averages.

3. Avoid the "Anytime TD" Addiction

Anytime Touchdown scorers are the most popular SGP leg, but they are also the most juiced. Instead of picking three different TD scorers, try combining one TD scorer with a "Yardage Over" for the same player. This creates better correlation and often a better price.

NBA Same Game Parlay Strategy

NBA SGPs move fast and are heavily reliant on "stat-stuffing."

1. The Triple-Double Correlation

If you are betting on a player like Nikola Jokic or Luka Doncic, their points, rebounds, and assists are highly correlated. If they are scoring well, they are drawing defenders, leading to more assists. If they are rebounding well, they are starting the fast break.

2. Tracking Pace of Play

Pace is the most important metric in NBA betting. Two fast-paced teams (like the Pacers or Kings) will generate more possessions, making "Over" legs on points, rebounds, and assists more likely to hit.

Advanced Tactics for Sharp Bettors

1. Line Shopping Across Books

Not all SGPs are created equal. FanDuel might offer +450 for a three-leg Chiefs SGP, while DraftKings offers +520 for the exact same legs. Over a long season, these differences in "price" are the difference between being a winning bettor and a losing one. Check our Odds Converter to compare different formats if you use international books.

2. Managing Your Bankroll

SGPs should be treated as "fun" bets, not your primary source of income. A common rule is to never wager more than 1-2% of your total bankroll on a single SGP. For more on this, see our guide to Bankroll Management Strategies.

3. The Kelly Criterion for SGPs

If you believe you have found a significant edge in a Same Game Parlay, you can use the Kelly Criterion Calculator to determine the mathematically optimal amount to bet. However, be cautious: because SGPs have higher volatility, many sharps use a "Fractional Kelly" to protect against swings.

Common SGP Mistakes to Avoid

Chasing "Boosts" blindly

Sportsbooks frequently offer "SGP Boosts" (e.g., +25% profit boost). While these can be valuable, they are often used to entice bettors into picking bad lines. Always build your SGP first, then see if the boost makes it a "value" play.

Over-complicating the Bet

It’s tempting to build a 12-leg SGP for a 1000-to-1 payout. In reality, these are almost always "donations" to the sportsbook. Stick to 2-4 legs where you have a high degree of confidence in the correlation. If you find yourself adding "just one more leg" to hit a certain payout, stop. You are likely falling for the Progression Betting trap.

FAQ: Same Game Parlay Strategy

Are Same Game Parlays better than regular parlays?

Mathematically, no. Sportsbooks usually charge a higher premium (vig) for SGPs because they have to account for correlation. However, SGPs allow you to use your knowledge of a specific team or player to create a "narrative" bet that a regular parlay cannot match.

Can you use a parlay calculator for SGPs?

Standard parlay calculators work by multiplying the odds of independent events. Because SGP legs are dependent, a standard calculator won't give you the exact odds the sportsbook offers, but it will help you see how much "correlation tax" the book is charging you.

What is the most profitable SGP leg?

There is no single "best" leg, but yardage "Overs" for wide receivers in high-total games tend to have some of the best historical correlation with team victories and quarterback performance.

How does "SGP Insurance" work?

Many books offer "SGP Insurance" where you get your stake back (usually in bonus bets) if exactly one leg loses. This is one of the few times an SGP can become mathematically favorable for the player.
