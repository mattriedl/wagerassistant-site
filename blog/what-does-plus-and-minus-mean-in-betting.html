Key takeaways

  • Minus (-) marks the favorite: the number is what you risk to win $100.
  • Plus (+) marks the underdog: the number is what a $100 bet wins.
  • +100 is even money; -110 on both sides is the standard spread and total price.
  • Implied probability: minus odds = odds / (odds + 100); plus odds = 100 / (odds + 100).
  • A winning bet returns your stake plus the profit shown by the odds.

In betting, a minus sign (−) marks the favorite and tells you how much you must bet to win $100, while a plus sign (+) marks the underdog and tells you how much you win on a $100 bet. So −150 means risk $150 to profit $100, and +150 means risk $100 to profit $150.

Those signs are the heart of American odds, the format every legal US sportsbook shows by default. If you have ever searched "what does and mean in betting," you were probably typing the + and − symbols, which search engines tend to strip out. This guide fixes that confusion for good.

By the end you will be able to read any plus or minus price, work out the payout for any stake, spot the favorite and the underdog at a glance, and turn odds into a win probability. Every number below is worked out step by step, and you can check your own with the free Moneyline Calculator.

Quick answer: A minus number (like −150) is the favorite: it is how much you must bet to win $100 in profit. A plus number (like +150) is the underdog: it is how much profit a $100 bet wins. Win, and you also get your original stake back. The bigger the minus number, the bigger the favorite; the bigger the plus number, the bigger the underdog.

What does plus and minus mean in betting?

American odds (also called moneyline odds or US odds) are built around a $100 reference bet. The sign in front of the number tells you which way that $100 works:

  • Minus (−) = favorite. The number is what you must risk to win $100 in profit. DraftKings' own betting guide puts it simply: for the favorite, "the odds number shows you how much you would have to risk to win $100" (DraftKings Sports Betting Guide).
  • Plus (+) = underdog. The number is what you win in profit on a $100 bet. The same guide says the underdog's number "is how much you would win for every $100 you wager."

Take a game listed like this: Team A −150, Team B +130. Team A is the favorite, because it carries the minus sign. Team B is the underdog. A $150 bet on Team A wins $100 in profit; a $100 bet on Team B wins $130 in profit.

You do not have to bet $100 or $150. The $100 is just the yardstick. Any stake scales up or down from it, as the examples below show.

Plus minus odds in one sentence

If you only remember one line: minus is what you risk to win $100, plus is what you win when you risk $100. Everything else, from spreads to parlays, builds on that rule.

What does minus mean in betting?

A negative number in betting means that side is expected to win more often than not, so the sportsbook pays you less than you risk. Your profit is smaller than your stake.

Here is how to read a few common minus prices:

  • −110: bet $110 to win $100. Profit on a $100 bet: $90.91.
  • −150: bet $150 to win $100. Profit on a $100 bet: $66.67.
  • −200: bet $200 to win $100. Profit on a $100 bet: $50.00.
  • −500: bet $500 to win $100. Profit on a $100 bet: $20.00.

Worked example: $20 on a −150 favorite

The formula for minus odds is: profit = stake × 100 ÷ the odds number (ignore the minus sign).

  1. Stake: $20. Odds: −150.
  2. Profit = $20 × 100 ÷ 150 = $13.33.
  3. Total return if it wins = $20 stake + $13.33 profit = $33.33.
  4. If it loses, you lose the $20.

Notice the trade-off. Favorites win more often, but each win pays less than you put at risk, so a single loss can wipe out several wins.

What does plus mean in betting?

A plus sign means the side is the underdog: the sportsbook thinks it is less likely to win, so it pays more than you risk. That is why bettors call any plus price "plus money."

  • +150: a $100 bet wins $150 in profit and returns $250 in total.
  • +200: a $100 bet wins $200 in profit and returns $300 in total. Put another way, +200 pays 2-to-1.
  • +500: a $100 bet wins $500 in profit and returns $600 in total.

Worked example: $10 on a +200 underdog

The formula for plus odds is: profit = stake × the odds number ÷ 100.

  1. Stake: $10. Odds: +200.
  2. Profit = $10 × 200 ÷ 100 = $20.00.
  3. Total return if it wins = $10 + $20 = $30.00.

Compare that with a −200 favorite: the same $10 would win only $5.00. Same number, opposite sign, very different payout.

Hard Rock Bet's odds guide makes a point worth repeating: "Your total return always includes your original bet plus any profit" (Hard Rock Bet). When a bet slip shows "payout" or "to return," it usually means stake plus profit; "to win" usually means profit only. Labels vary a little by sportsbook, so check which one you are reading.

Why do spreads and totals show −110?

Plus and minus signs do two different jobs on a betting board, and that trips up a lot of new bettors. A point spread line often looks like this:

  • Team A −3.5 (−110)
  • Team B +3.5 (−110)

The first number is the spread: Team A must win by 4 or more points, and Team B covers if it wins outright or loses by 3 or fewer. The number in brackets is the price, read exactly like a moneyline: bet $110 to win $100 on either side. DraftKings' guide uses the same example: "If the odds offered on a particular selection are −110, you would need to bet $110 to profit $100."

So a minus spread number means "give points," while a minus price means "risk more than you win." For a closer look at the most common spread number in baseball and hockey, see what −1.5 means in betting. Totals work the same way: "Over 47.5 (−110)" uses −110 as the price, as explained in our over/under guide. To test a spread with your own numbers, try the Spread Calculator.

Why −110 on both sides instead of +100? That extra $10 per $100 is the sportsbook's built-in fee, called the vig or juice. Our guide to what vig is shows how it is calculated.

What do +100, even money and pick'em mean?

+100 is even money. You win exactly what you risk: $100 profit on a $100 bet, or 2.00 in decimal odds. DraftKings defines even money as "a bet that returns as much profit as the initial wager was." You may also see it written as "even" on some boards; it is not the same as EV meaning expected value, a different idea covered in our Expected Value Calculator.

−100 and +100 are the same price. Both mean risk $100 to win $100. The DraftKings guide lists even money as +100 in American odds.

Pick'em (PK) means there is no favorite or underdog on the spread, so the game is effectively decided straight up. According to the DraftKings guide, a pick'em is "usually displayed with both teams at −110." That is why you can see two minus prices in the same game: both sides are close to a coin flip, and the vig pushes both just below even money. The same thing happens on moneylines for evenly matched teams, such as −115 and −105.

What do plus and minus odds say about each team's chances?

Every price can be turned into an implied probability: the win chance the odds suggest, before you account for the vig.

  • Minus odds: probability = odds ÷ (odds + 100). For −150: 150 ÷ 250 = 60.0%.
  • Plus odds: probability = 100 ÷ (odds + 100). For +130: 100 ÷ 230 = 43.5%.

Add those two together for the Team A −150 / Team B +130 game and you get 103.5%, not 100%. The extra 3.5% is the sportsbook's margin. Strip it out and the fair, no-vig chances are about 58.0% for Team A and 42.0% for Team B.

How do you calculate the payout on plus or minus odds?

You can work out any American-odds payout in four steps:

  1. Check the sign. Minus means favorite; plus means underdog.
  2. For minus odds, divide 100 by the number and multiply by your stake. $25 at −110: 100 ÷ 110 × $25 = $22.73 profit.
  3. For plus odds, divide the number by 100 and multiply by your stake. $25 at +150: 150 ÷ 100 × $25 = $37.50 profit.
  4. Add your stake back to get the total return: $47.73 and $62.50 in these examples.

Parlays combine prices by multiplying their decimal odds, so plus and minus legs mix freely. Our parlay betting tips walk through that math.

What mistakes do beginners make with plus and minus odds?

  • Reading the spread as the price. In "−3.5 (−110)," the −3.5 is points and the −110 is the payout. Different jobs.
  • Thinking you must bet $100 or $150. The $100 is only the reference. A $5 bet at −150 is fine; it just wins $3.33.
  • Assuming favorites are safe. A −300 favorite has an implied chance of 75.0%, which still means it loses about one time in four on the book's own numbers.
  • Chasing big plus numbers. +500 looks exciting, but the implied chance is only 16.7%. Plus money is not better value by itself; it just pays more because it wins less often.
  • Mixing up "to win" and "payout." One is profit, the other includes your stake.
  • Ignoring the vig. When both sides of a bet are −110, you need to win 52.4% of the time just to break even.
  • Not comparing prices. −110 and −120 on the same bet are different deals: $90.91 versus $83.33 profit on $100. Legal sportsbooks in different states can post different numbers.

Legal Sports Report sums up the sign rule the same way most sportsbooks do: "negative numbers indicate favorites" and "positive numbers indicate underdogs" (Legal Sports Report). Once that clicks, every line on the board starts to make sense.
