What Does Over/Under Mean in Betting? Totals Explained With Examples

Key takeaways

  • Over/under (a total or O/U) is a bet on the combined score of both teams, not on who wins.
  • A .5 line can't push; a whole-number line refunds your stake if the total lands exactly on it.
  • Full-game US totals usually include overtime, and an NHL shootout adds one goal to the winner.
  • Soccer totals are settled on 90 minutes plus stoppage time; extra time and penalties don't count.
  • At -110 you need to win 52.4% of bets to break even; the book's hold is about 4.55%.

In betting, over/under means you are wagering on whether the combined score of a game will finish above or below a number the sportsbook sets, not on who wins. If the total is 44.5 and the final is 27-17, that is 44 points, so the under wins and the over loses.

This guide explains what over/under means in betting for the NFL, NBA, MLB, NHL and soccer, why most totals end in .5, what happens on a push, how overtime and shootouts are counted, and how to read the odds next to the number. By the end you can read any totals line, work out the payout and know how your bet will be settled.

Quick answer: An over/under (also called a total or O/U) is a bet on the combined points, runs or goals both teams score. You pick Over if you think the final total will be higher than the sportsbook's line, or Under if you think it will be lower. The winner of the game doesn't matter.

What does over/under mean in betting?

An over/under is a two-way bet on a number. The sportsbook posts a total for a game, such as 44.5 points, and you choose one side:

  • Over: you win if the two teams combine for more than the line.
  • Under: you win if they combine for fewer than the line.

That is why people also search "what does under and over mean in betting" and "what does total mean in betting": over/under, O/U and totals are the same market. Wikipedia's over–under entry describes it the same way: the book predicts a number for a statistic, and bettors wager on whether the actual result lands above or below it.

The same idea applies to more than the final score. Books post totals for halves, quarters, periods, one team's score (a team total) and player stats such as passing yards or points (player props). If you want to go deeper on player markets, see our prop betting strategy guide.

How does over/under betting work?

Here is how a totals bet works from start to finish:

  1. The sportsbook sets a total, for example Over 44.5 (-110) / Under 44.5 (-110).
  2. You pick Over or Under and choose your stake.
  3. After the game, add both teams' final scores together.
  4. Compare that sum with the line. Above it, the over wins. Below it, the under wins. Exactly on a whole-number line, the bet is a push and your stake comes back.

The number in brackets is the price. At -110 you risk $110 to win $100, or $100 to win $90.91. You can check any price instantly with the Over/Under Calculator.

Worked example: NFL total

Say an NFL game has a total of 44.5 and you bet $100 on the over at -110.

  • Final score 27-20: 47 points. 47 is above 44.5, so the over wins. You get back $190.91 ($100 stake plus $90.91 profit).
  • Final score 27-17: 44 points. 44 is below 44.5, so the over loses $100.

The photo at the top of this page shows the same idea: Yale 27, Cornell 16 is 43 combined points. Against a 44.5 total the under would win; against 42.5 the over would win.

What does 2.5, 44.5 or any half point mean in betting?

A half point (the "hook") on a total means the game can't land exactly on the line, because teams can't score half a point, run or goal. So every bet on a .5 total has a winner and a loser and no push.

In soccer and hockey you will see small lines like 1.5, 2.5 and 5.5. "What does 2.5 mean in betting?" usually comes up in soccer: Over 2.5 goals needs three or more goals in total, and Under 2.5 goals needs two or fewer. A 2-1 or 3-0 game is over; 1-1, 2-0 or 0-0 is under.

Some soccer books also offer quarter-goal Asian totals such as 2.25 or 2.75, which split your stake across two lines. Those work like Asian handicaps, which we cover in our Asian handicap guide.

What happens if the over/under lands exactly on the number?

If the line is a whole number and the final total matches it, the bet is a push: neither side wins and your stake is refunded. DraftKings' house rules give this exact example: an over 100 total points bet on a game that ends 60-40 is settled as a push, and a push returns only the stake (DraftKings Sportsbook House Rules, Massachusetts).

In a parlay, a pushed total usually drops out and the parlay is paid on the remaining legs at reduced odds. DraftKings says the stake stays on the parlay "with the remaining number of legs." Here is what that does to a $100 three-leg parlay with every leg at -110:

ScenarioLegs that countTotal return on $100
All three legs win3$695.79
Two win, the total pushes2$364.46

Teasers and same game parlays can follow different push rules, and rules differ between books, so check your sportsbook's house rules before you bet. Our Parlay Calculator shows the reduced payout if a leg pushes.

Does overtime count in an over/under bet?

For full-game totals in US sports, usually yes. Period, quarter and half totals usually don't include overtime. Soccer is the big exception. Here is how two major US sportsbooks write it in their rules:

SportFull-game total includesNotes
NFL and college footballOvertimeDraftKings: "All bets include overtime statistics."
NBA and college basketballOvertimeQuarter totals exclude overtime.
NHLOvertime and a shootoutA shootout adds one goal to the winning team's score.
MLBExtra inningsShortened games may be voided; see below.
Soccer90 minutes plus stoppage time onlyExtra time and penalty shootouts don't count.

The hockey rule surprises a lot of bettors. DraftKings says that if a game is decided by a shootout, one goal is added to the winner's score and to the game's total goals for settlement. So a 3-3 game that goes to a shootout settles as 4-3, which is 7 goals: Over 6.5 wins, and a total of 6 (which would have pushed at 3-3) becomes an over winner.

For soccer, FanDuel says bets are settled on 90 minutes of play, which "includes time added on by referee for stoppages," and that extra time and penalty shootouts are excluded unless a market says otherwise (FanDuel Sportsbook House Rules, Ohio). A cup tie that is 1-1 after 90 minutes is under 2.5 goals, even if someone scores in extra time.

Baseball: shortened games and pitchers

Full-game baseball totals include extra innings. Many US books require a game to go nine innings (or 8.5 if the home team is ahead) for a total to have action; if a game is called early, the total is often voided unless the result was already decided. For example, DraftKings voids total runs bets on a game ended by a mercy rule unless settlement was already "Unconditionally Determined." Some books let you choose "listed pitchers," which voids the bet if a named starter doesn't start, while DraftKings doesn't void game bets solely because the starting pitchers change. Rules vary by book and state, so read the baseball section of your book's house rules.

How do you read over/under odds and payouts?

A totals line has two parts: the number (44.5) and the price on each side (-110). The number decides who wins; the price decides how much you win. When both sides are -110, the book thinks the over and under are about equally likely and keeps a margin for itself.

Prices often differ between the two sides, for example Over 8.5 (-115) / Under 8.5 (-105). Here is what common totals prices pay on a $100 stake:

How much does the sportsbook charge on a total?

The book's cut is called the vig (or juice). With both sides at -110, the implied probabilities add up to 104.76% instead of 100%, which works out to a hold of about 4.55%. Put another way, if over and under were truly 50/50, a $100 bet at -110 would lose $4.55 on average.

For a line of Over 8.5 (-115) / Under 8.5 (-105), the implied probabilities are 53.49% and 51.22%, a total of 104.71%. Removing the vig gives fair probabilities of about 51.1% for the over and 48.9% for the under. You can do this for any line with the No-Vig Calculator, which helps you compare prices across books and spot when one side is shaded.

What types of over/under bets can you make?

Most US sportsbooks offer several kinds of totals bets on the same game:

  • Game total: combined score of both teams for the full game.
  • Team total: one team's score only, for example a team over 23.5 points.
  • Half, quarter and period totals: just one part of the game (usually no overtime, per the rules above).
  • Alternate totals: the same game at different numbers with different prices, such as Over 41.5 at a shorter price or Over 50.5 at a longer price.
  • Player totals (props): a single player's yards, points, strikeouts or shots.
  • Live totals: lines that update during the game as the score and clock change.

Some books also let you buy half a point on a total, for example moving Under 44 to Under 44.5 for a worse price. The Half-Point Calculator shows whether the extra half point is worth the cost.

How do totals differ in the NFL, NBA, MLB, NHL and soccer?

The concept is identical in every sport. What changes is the size of the number and how often scores land on particular totals.

What mistakes should you avoid with over/under bets?

  • Forgetting overtime and shootouts. An NHL under can lose in a shootout because the winner gets an extra goal for settlement.
  • Assuming soccer extra time counts. Standard totals stop at the end of 90 minutes plus stoppage time.
  • Ignoring the price. Over 44.5 at -125 needs a 55.6% win rate to break even; at -105 it needs 51.2%.
  • Not shopping lines. Half a point or a few cents of juice between books adds up over a season.
  • Stacking totals in parlays without knowing the push rule. A pushed total usually shrinks your parlay payout.
  • Chasing losses. Decide your stake size first; our bankroll management guide explains how.

Totals aren't easier or harder than sides by nature; you still pay the vig on every bet. Finding value means estimating the total more accurately than the posted line, which our value betting guide covers.

Putting it together

Over/under means one thing in every sport: add up both teams' score and compare it with the line. Half-point totals always have a winner, whole-number totals can push, and the fine print on overtime, shootouts, extra time and shortened games decides close cases.
