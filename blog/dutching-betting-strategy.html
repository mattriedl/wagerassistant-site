Have you ever spent hours analyzing a horse race or a soccer match, narrowed it down to two or three likely winners, only to pick the wrong one? It’s one of the most frustrating experiences in gambling—being "right" about the contenders but "wrong" about the specific result.

Enter the Dutching Betting Strategy.

Dutching is a sophisticated staking method that allows you to back multiple outcomes in a single event so that you realize the same profit regardless of which of your selections wins. Instead of gambling on a single outcome, you are essentially "buying" a larger portion of the market.

In this comprehensive guide, we’ll dive deep into the math, strategies, and tips you need to master dutching and turn it into a consistent part of your betting arsenal.

What is the Dutching Betting Strategy?

Dutching is the practice of placing multiple bets within a single market so that the total return is the same if any of your chosen selections win. While most casual bettors place a single "back" bet on a favorite, a dutcher might back two, three, or even four different outcomes simultaneously.

The name is widely attributed to Dutch Schultz, a notorious 1930s mobster and racketeer who reportedly used this mathematical approach to protect his bankroll at the racetrack.

Unlike a standard parlay (where you need every leg to win), dutching only requires one of your selected outcomes to hit. If any horse or score you backed wins, you collect your profit. If an outcome you didn't back occurs, you lose your total combined stake.

The Core Concept: Coverage At its heart, dutching is about coverage. By spreading your risk across multiple outcomes, you increase your strike rate (the frequency of winning bets) while reducing the volatility of your bankroll. It is a mathematical way of saying, "I know the winner is in this group, but I’m not sure exactly which one it is."

How Dutching Works: The Math Explained

To understand dutching, you have to move away from looking at "odds" and start looking at implied probability. Every set of odds—whether American, decimal, or fractional—represents the likelihood of an event occurring.

You can use our Odds Converter to quickly switch between formats, but for the math of dutching, decimal odds are the easiest to work with.

The Dutching Formula To calculate how much to stake on each selection to ensure an equal profit, you follow these steps:

  1. Calculate Implied Probability: Divide 1 by the decimal odds for each selection. * *Example: Selection A at 4.00 has an implied probability of 1/4.00 = 0.25 (25%).*
  2. Sum the Probabilities: Add up the implied probabilities of all your selections. * *Example: Selection A (25%) + Selection B (20%) = 45%.*
  3. Calculate Individual Stakes: To get the stake for each selection, use this formula: * *Stake = (Selection Probability / Total Probability) x Total Budget*

A Practical Example: Soccer Correct Score Imagine you are betting on a low-scoring soccer match between two defensive teams. You believe the match will end in a 1-0, 2-0, or 2-1 victory for the home team.

The odds are: * 1-0: 7.00 (14.3% probability) * 2-0: 9.00 (11.1% probability) * 2-1: 8.50 (11.8% probability)

Total Probability: 14.3% + 11.1% + 11.8% = 37.2%.

If your total budget for this game is $100, the stakes would be: * 1-0 Stake: (14.3 / 37.2) x $100 = $38.44 * 2-0 Stake: (11.1 / 37.2) x $100 = $29.84 * 2-1 Stake: (11.8 / 37.2) x $100 = $31.72

No matter which of those three scores hits, your total return will be approximately $269 (a $169 profit). This is much safer than picking just one score and losing if the game ends in a different—but still predictable—result.

Dutching vs. Arbitrage: Understanding the Gap

It is common to confuse Dutching with Arbitrage Betting. While they both use proportional staking, their goals and risk profiles are fundamentally different.

FeatureDutchingArbitrage (Arbing)
CoverageUsually a subset of outcomes.All possible outcomes.
RiskYou lose if the "outsider" wins.Theoretically risk-free.
Profit MarginHigh potential profit.Very low (usually 1-3%).
SustainabilityLess likely to get you banned.High risk of bookmaker limits.

Arbitrage is a specialized form of dutching where the total implied probability across all bookmakers is less than 100%, guaranteeing a profit. Traditional dutching is used by value bettors who have a "lean" on a market but want to cover multiple bases within that lean.

Popular Dutching Strategies for Major Sports

While you can dutch almost any market, certain sports lend themselves perfectly to this strategy.

1. Horse Racing: The "Top 2" Strategy In horse racing, the favorite only wins about 33% of the time. However, the top two or three horses in the betting market often account for 60-70% of all winners. By dutching the top two favorites in a competitive race, you can maintain a high win rate while still securing a profit, provided the combined odds offer value.

2. Soccer: The "Insurance" Dutch Soccer offers several markets where dutching is king. The Correct Score market is the most popular, but many professionals use dutching for Match Outcome + Both Teams to Score (BTTS). For example, if you think a team will win but aren't sure if they will keep a clean sheet, you can dutch "Home Win to Nil" and "Home Win and BTTS."

3. Golf: Outright Winner Golf fields are massive, often featuring 144+ players. Picking a single winner is nearly impossible. Professional golf bettors often dutch 5 to 10 players in a single tournament. Even with 10 selections, because golf odds are so high (often 20/1 to 100/1), a single winner in your "stable" can lead to a massive payday.

Why You Should Use a Dutching Calculator

Manually calculating implied probabilities and proportional stakes is a recipe for disaster. A single rounding error or a mistyped digit can lead to a "unbalanced" dutch where you lose money even if your selection wins.

A good calculator helps you find the closest "round" numbers that still maintain a fair profit distribution.

Advantages and Risks of Dutching

The Advantages * Higher Win Rate: Psychologically, winning 60% of your bets is much easier to handle than winning 20%. * Lower Variance: Your bankroll won't see the massive swings associated with high-odds single bets. * Value Compounding: If you find three horses that are all undervalued by the bookie, dutching them together amplifies your mathematical edge. Use our Expected Value Calculator to find these edges.

The Risks * Compounded Losses: When you lose a dutch, you lose your *entire* combined stake. This can be significantly more than a single flat bet. * The "Longshot" Hazard: If a massive outsider wins, all your sophisticated math counts for nothing. * Stake Sizing: You need a larger bankroll to support multiple bets per event. This is where Bankroll Management and tools like the Kelly Criterion Calculator become vital.

5 Expert Tips for Successful Dutching

1. Identify Value First Dutching is a staking tool, not a crystal ball. It doesn't magically create an edge where none exists. Before you dutch, you must be confident that the combined probability of your selections is higher than what the odds suggest.

2. Limit Your Selections While it's tempting to cover 6 horses in a 10-horse race, the more outcomes you add, the lower your profit margin becomes. Stick to 2-4 selections to ensure the reward is worth the risk.

3. Use Rounded Stakes Bookmakers use automated software to flag "professional" bettors. Betting $14.53 is a red flag. Betting $15.00 is what recreational players do. Always round your stakes to the nearest $1 or $5 to keep your accounts healthy.

4. Diversify Across Sports Don't just stick to horse racing. Look at tennis (dutching exact set scores) or baseball (dutching the run line and moneyline) to find untapped value.

5. Always Check for "No-Vig" Odds Before placing your bets, try to understand the "fair" price of the market. Stripping away the bookmaker’s margin (the vigorish) helps you see if your dutch actually has a positive expected value.
