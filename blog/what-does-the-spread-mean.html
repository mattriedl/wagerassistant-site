What Does the Spread Mean in Betting? Point Spreads Explained

Key takeaways

  • The spread is a points handicap: the favorite (minus) must win by more than the number, and the underdog (plus) can lose by less or win outright.
  • Covering the spread means your side beat the number; the game winner and the bet winner can be different teams.
  • Most spreads are priced at -110, so a $50 bet wins $45.45 and you need to win 52.4% of bets to break even.
  • If the margin lands exactly on a whole-number spread, the bet is a push and your stake is refunded; a .5 hook prevents pushes.
  • Overtime, tie and parlay push rules vary by sportsbook and state, so check the house rules.

In sports betting, the spread is a head start in points that the sportsbook gives the underdog and takes away from the favorite, so both sides become roughly even bets. If you bet the favorite at -6.5, your team has to win by 7 or more; if you bet the underdog at +6.5, your team can lose by 6 or fewer, or win outright, and you still cash.

That's what the spread means in betting: you aren't just picking who wins, you're picking who wins once the points are added or subtracted. It's the most common bet on NFL, college football and NBA games, and with a full Thanksgiving slate and the college football rivalry weekend coming up, it's the number you'll see first next to every matchup.

This guide explains how to read a point spread, what covering the spread and "against the spread" mean, exactly what a spread bet pays at -110, and what happens when the final margin lands right on the number. You can check every payout in this article with the free Point Spread Calculator.

Quick answer: The spread (or point spread) is the margin of victory a sportsbook sets to even out a game. The favorite has a minus number, like -6.5, and must win by more than that. The underdog has a plus number, like +6.5, and wins the bet by losing by less or winning outright. Most spreads are priced around -110.

What does the spread mean in betting?

The point spread is the sportsbook's handicap for a game. Caesars Sportsbook defines it as "a margin of victory assigned to the favored team that determines the outcome of a spread wager" (Caesars sports betting glossary). In plain terms, the book estimates how much better one team is and sets a number that should attract betting on both sides.

Here's how that works on a hypothetical NFL game:

  • Team A -6.5 (-110): Team A is the favorite. Subtract 6.5 points from its final score. It must win by 7 or more for a bet on Team A to win.
  • Team B +6.5 (-110): Team B is the underdog. Add 6.5 points to its final score. A bet on Team B wins if it loses by 6 or fewer, or wins the game.

The spread is the same number with opposite signs on each side. The second number in brackets, -110, is the price (the odds) you pay for the bet. It's separate from the spread itself, and it's covered in the payout section below.

You'll also see the spread called "the line," "ATS" (against the spread), or a "handicap" in other countries. Hockey and baseball use a fixed spread of 1.5 called the puck line and run line; our guide to what -1.5 means in betting covers those.

How do you read a point spread?

A spread listing on a sportsbook usually shows three things for each team: the team name, the spread, and the odds. Read it in this order:

  1. Find the minus sign. The team with the minus spread (for example -6.5) is the favorite. That's what minus means in a betting spread: points taken away from that team's score.
  2. Find the plus sign. The team with the plus spread (+6.5) is the underdog. Those points are added to its score.
  3. Read the odds next to it. -110 means you risk $110 to win $100. If you need a refresher on minus and plus odds, see what plus and minus mean in betting.
  4. Adjust the final score. After the game, add or subtract the spread from your team's score. If your team is ahead after the adjustment, your bet wins.

Two special cases come up often. A pick'em (written PK or "pick") is a game with no spread at all: Caesars describes it as a game "in which neither team is assigned a point spread," so you simply need your team to win. And a spread ending in .5, like 6.5, is said to have a hook, the extra half point that makes a tie with the spread impossible.

What does it mean to cover the spread?

Covering the spread means your side has done enough against the number for the bet to win. Caesars puts it this way: "To cover the spread means a team has met the requirement for bettors on that side to win their spread wagers" (Caesars sports betting glossary).

  • A favorite covers when it wins by more than the spread.
  • An underdog covers when it loses by less than the spread or wins the game outright.

What does against the spread mean?

"Against the spread," usually shortened to ATS, means a result measured with the point spread applied rather than by who won the game. Betting a team ATS is the same thing as betting it on the spread.

You'll mostly see ATS in team records. A team that is 7-3 ATS has covered the spread in 7 games and failed to cover in 3, no matter its actual win-loss record. A heavy favorite can go 9-1 straight up (SU) and only 4-6 ATS, because winning isn't enough when it's expected to win big. Pushes are usually shown as a third number, such as 6-3-1 ATS.

ATS records describe what already happened. They don't tell you what will happen next, because sportsbooks adjust future spreads as teams get better or worse. For more terms like SU, chalk and fading, see our sports betting terms glossary.

How much does a spread bet pay?

Most spreads are priced at -110 on both sides, sometimes called "laying 11 to win 10." You risk $110 to win $100 in profit, and a winning bet returns $210 (your $110 stake plus $100). The formula for minus odds is: profit = stake × (100 ÷ odds).

At -110:

  • $110 bet wins $100.00 profit, $210.00 returned.
  • $100 bet wins $90.91 profit, $190.91 returned.
  • $50 bet wins $45.45 profit, $95.45 returned.
  • $20 bet wins $18.18 profit, $38.18 returned.

Spreads aren't always -110. Books often move the price instead of the spread, so you might see -3 (-120) on one side and +3 (+100) on the other. Here's what a $50 bet pays at common spread prices:

The last column is the implied probability of the odds: the share of bets you'd need to win just to break even. At -110 that's 52.4%, not 50%, because the sportsbook charges a fee called the vig. If you won exactly half of your $110 bets at -110, you would lose $5 per bet on average, about 4.5% of what you risk. Spread parlays

You can also combine spread bets in a parlay, where every leg must cover. Two -110 legs pay about +264 (a $10 parlay returns $36.45), three legs pay about +596 ($69.58 back on $10), and four legs pay about +1228 ($132.83 back on $10). Run your own combinations in the Parlay Calculator.

What happens if the game lands exactly on the spread?

When the spread is a whole number and the final margin matches it exactly, the bet is a push. Caesars explains that a push "occurs when the final result lands exactly on the posted point spread or total. All stakes are returned" (Caesars sports betting glossary). So if you bet Team A -7 and it wins by exactly 7, you get your stake back with no profit and no loss.

DraftKings' house rules give the same example: on a 2-way point spread market with a three-point spread, a game ending 13-10 is settled as a push, and only the stake is returned (DraftKings Massachusetts house rules). That's why many spreads end in .5. The hook removes the chance of a tie with the number, so every bet either wins or loses.

A few settlement details are worth knowing, because they vary by sportsbook and state:

  • Overtime: most US sportsbooks count overtime on NFL, college football and NBA spreads, but some markets (such as quarter, half or 3-way lines) use regulation only. Check the house rules for the market you bet.
  • Ties in the NFL: regular-season NFL games can still end in a tie. The NFL's rules say that if the score is still level after the overtime period, "the result of the game will be recorded as a tie" (NFL overtime rules). Against a spread, a tie simply means the favorite failed to cover.
  • Pushes in parlays: a push usually doesn't refund a parlay. DraftKings, for example, keeps the stake on the parlay and settles it on the remaining legs, which lowers the payout (DraftKings Massachusetts house rules).
  • Your line is locked: you get the spread and odds shown on your ticket when the bet is accepted, even if the line moves later.

Because a half point can turn a push into a win or a loss, it has real value. The Half Point Calculator shows what buying an extra half point costs and when it might be worth it.

What does the spread mean in football and the NBA?

The spread means the same thing in every sport, but the typical numbers look different because of how each sport scores.

NFL and college football

In the NFL, spreads commonly range from pick'em to around two touchdowns. Football scores come mostly in 3s (field goals) and 7s (touchdowns with an extra point), so final margins of 3 and 7 come up often. That's why the difference between -2.5 and -3.5, or -6.5 and -7.5, matters so much: one half point can decide whether you win, lose or push. College football spreads can be much larger, sometimes 20 points or more, because the gap between teams is often wider.

NBA and college basketball

In the NBA, the spread works the same way, and you'll often see spreads anywhere from 1.5 to double digits. Basketball has many more scoring plays, so margins are spread more evenly and no single number carries the weight that 3 and 7 do in football. A late free throw or a meaningless basket at the buzzer can still flip the result against the spread, which is why basketball bettors pay close attention to the exact number.

Other sports

Hockey and baseball usually use a standard 1.5 spread (the puck line and run line), and soccer uses goal handicaps, including Asian handicaps with quarter-goal lines. The Asian Handicap Calculator settles those split lines for you.

What's the difference between the spread and the moneyline?

A moneyline bet only asks who wins the game. A spread bet asks who wins after the handicap. The table compares the two on the same hypothetical game:

BetTeam A (favorite)Team B (underdog)
Spread-6.5 at -110: must win by 7++6.5 at -110: can lose by up to 6
MoneylineJust needs to win, at a shorter priceMust win outright, at a bigger price
$50 at -110 on the spread$45.45 profit if A covers$45.45 profit if B covers

The spread makes both sides cost about the same, while the moneyline makes you pay more to back the favorite and pays more for the underdog. Neither is better in general: they're two ways of pricing the same game. Our guide to what moneyline means in betting covers that side, and the Moneyline Calculator handles the math.

Why does the spread change before a game?

The opening spread is the sportsbook's first number, and it can move all week. Common reasons include injury news, weather, lineup changes and the amount and type of money bet on each side. A move from -6.5 to -7.5 means the favorite now has to win by 8 instead of 7.

Different sportsbooks can post different spreads on the same game at the same time, so comparing books before you bet can get you a better number or a better price. What are common mistakes with spread bets?

  • Confusing winning with covering. A favorite that wins by less than the spread loses the bet. Always adjust the score first.
  • Ignoring the price. -6.5 at -120 is a different bet from -6.5 at -110. The higher price needs a 54.5% win rate to break even instead of 52.4%.
  • Not checking the number on your ticket. The spread you get is the one shown when your bet is accepted, not the one posted later.
  • Forgetting overtime and push rules. Most full-game spreads include overtime, and pushes refund straight bets but shrink parlays. Rules vary by sportsbook and state.
  • Treating ATS records as predictions. Past cover rates don't account for how the spread itself has changed.

Once you can read a spread, you can read most of a sportsbook's game menu: the favorite gives points, the underdog gets them, and the price tells you what a win pays.
