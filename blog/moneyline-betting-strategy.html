Moneyline Betting Strategy: Finding Value in Outright Wins

Moneyline betting is often the very first step for any sports bettor. It is the purest form of wagering: you pick a side, and if they win the game, you win your bet. There are no point spreads to cover and no total points to sweat. However, don't let its simplicity fool you. While the premise is straightforward, mastering a moneyline betting strategy is what separates the casual fans from the long-term profitable bettors.

In this comprehensive guide, we will dive deep into the mechanics of moneyline wagering, the mathematics of implied probability, and the advanced strategies you need to find a consistent edge in the market.

What is Moneyline Betting?

At its core, a moneyline bet is a wager on which team or athlete will win a contest outright. Unlike point spread betting—where a favorite must win by a certain margin and an underdog can lose by a small amount and still "cover"—the moneyline only cares about the final score.

Because not all teams are created equal, sportsbooks adjust the payouts rather than the score. If a heavy favorite like the Kansas City Chiefs plays a struggling team, the payout for betting on the Chiefs will be small, while a bet on the underdog will offer a significant return to compensate for the higher risk.

Reading Moneyline Odds: Favorites vs. Underdogs

To execute a successful moneyline betting strategy, you must first understand how to read the price. In the United States, we use American Odds, which are centered around a 00 baseline.

American Odds (+ and -)

  • The Favorite (-): Indicated by a minus sign. The number tells you how much you need to bet to win 00 in profit. For example, if a team is -150, you must wager 50 to make 00.
  • The Underdog (+): Indicated by a plus sign. The number tells you how much profit you will make on a 00 wager. For example, if a team is +130, a 00 bet earns you 30 in profit.

Understanding these numbers is the foundation of sports betting tips for beginners. But to move beyond the basics, we need to talk about probability.

Converting Odds to Probability

Every moneyline price reflects an "implied probability." This is the percentage chance the sportsbook thinks the event has of happening, plus their "vig" (commission).

  • Negative Odds Calculation: Odds / (Odds + 100)
  • Positive Odds Calculation: 100 / (Odds + 100)

If you see a team at -200, the implied probability is 66.7%. If you believe that team has a 75% chance of winning, you have found "value." You can quickly calculate these conversions using our Odds Converter.

The Math of the Moneyline: Finding Your Edge

The secret to a winning moneyline betting strategy isn't picking winners—it's picking "value." Value occurs when your assessed probability of an outcome is higher than the probability implied by the odds.

Expected Value (+EV) in Moneyline Bets

Expected Value (EV) is the most important concept in professional gambling. It measures how much you can expect to win or lose on average if you were to place the same bet hundreds of times.

The formula is: EV = (Probability of Winning * Profit) - (Probability of Losing * Stake)

If the result is positive, the bet is +EV and should be taken. If it is negative, you are essentially paying the sportsbook a premium to take your money. We recommend using our Expected Value Calculator to run these numbers before every wager.

Why the "Heavy Favorite" Strategy Often Fails

Many beginners fall into the trap of betting on heavy favorites (e.g., -400 or higher). They assume that because the team is "guaranteed" to win, it's free money. The problem is the math. At -400, you are risking 00 to win 00. You need to win 80% of the time just to break even. If the favorite loses just once in five tries, you are in the red. Sportsbooks often "shade" the lines on public favorites, making the implied probability higher than the actual win rate, which creates -EV scenarios.

Advanced Moneyline Strategies

Once you understand the math, you can apply specific tactics to boost your ROI.

Line Shopping for the Best Price

The most significant advantage a bettor has is the ability to shop around. Not every sportsbook offers the same moneyline. One book might have the underdog at +140, while another has them at +150. While a 0 difference might seem small on a single 00 bet, it adds up to thousands of dollars over a full season. Consistently getting the best price reduces the "hold"—the built-in commission the sportsbook takes—and is a core component of any professional moneyline betting strategy. In fact, if you aren't line shopping, you are effectively giving away 2-5% of your ROI every single day.

The Underdog Advantage

In parity-driven leagues like the NFL, MLB, or the NBA, "any given Sunday" is a reality. Because the betting public loves to back "winners" and household names, the odds on favorites are often pushed beyond their realistic win probability. This creates a "premium" that savvy bettors can exploit by backing underdogs.

Look for "live" underdogs—teams that have a statistical path to victory (e.g., a great defense playing a backup quarterback) but are being undervalued by the market due to recent losses or public perception. If a +150 underdog wins just 41% of the time, you are in the black. Contrast that with a -150 favorite who must win 61% of the time just to break even.

3-Way Moneyline Strategy (Soccer & NHL)

In sports like soccer, the "Draw" is a common outcome. A 3-way moneyline offers three options: Team A wins, Team B wins, or a Draw. The odds on a 3-way moneyline are usually much higher than a 2-way "Draw No Bet" or Asian Handicap market. For a detailed breakdown of how to navigate these split markets, see our guide on Asian Handicap Betting Strategy.

Moneyline Parlays: High Reward, High Risk

A popular moneyline betting strategy is "parlaying" two or more heavy favorites together to get a better payout. For instance, two -200 favorites combined result in a +125 payout. While this looks attractive, remember that you are compounding the sportsbook's vig. Every leg you add to a parlay increases the house edge significantly. Only use parlays when you are certain that both legs are +EV on their own. You can calculate the exact returns of these combinations with our Parlay Calculator.

Psychological Factors: Avoiding Tilt and the "Sure Thing" Bias

One of the biggest hurdles in successfully executing a moneyline betting strategy is the human brain. We are evolutionarily wired to avoid loss, which makes betting on underdogs feel "scary" and betting on favorites feel "safe."

  • The Sure Thing Bias: We tend to overvalue outcomes that seem certain. This leads to betting too much on favorites, where the risk-to-reward ratio is often poor.
  • The Recency Bias: If a team won by 30 points last week, the public will bet them heavily this week, regardless of the matchup. This "public hype" often inflates the moneyline price, creating value on the opposite side.
  • Handling "Tilt": After a heart-breaking loss on a moneyline bet, it's tempting to "chase" with a big wager on a heavy favorite just to win your money back. This is a recipe for disaster.

When to Walk Away: Evaluating the Hold

Every moneyline has a "hold"—the percentage of all wagers that the sportsbook keeps as profit. In major markets like the NFL, the hold might be as low as 3-4%. In niche markets, it can climb as high as 15%. Before you place a bet, calculate the "no-vig" odds to see what the actual fair price should be. If the gap between your assessed win probability and the no-vig odds is too small, the best strategy is often to simply walk away.

Key Takeaways

  • Moneyline is about Value, not Winners: Don't just ask who will win; ask if the payout is worth the risk.
  • Avoid the "Favorite Trap": Heavy favorites require an unsustainable win rate to remain profitable.
  • Calculate Implied Probability: Always convert odds to percentages to see the "true" price of the bet.
  • Line Shop Religiously: Always find the best price to minimize the house edge.

FAQ

What is a moneyline bet?

A moneyline bet is a wager on which team or player will win a game or match outright, with no point spread involved.

Is moneyline betting profitable?

Yes, it can be highly profitable if you focus on finding positive expected value (+EV) and avoid over-paying for popular favorites.

How do you calculate moneyline payouts?

For negative odds (-), divide 100 by the odds and multiply by your stake. For positive odds (+), divide the odds by 100 and multiply by your stake.

What is the difference between moneyline and spread betting?

In a moneyline bet, you only need your team to win. In spread betting, your team must win by a certain number of points (favorite) or not lose by more than a certain number of points (underdog).

What is a 3-way moneyline?

A 3-way moneyline includes the "Draw" as a third betting option, commonly found in soccer and some hockey markets.

How do you find value in moneyline odds?

Value is found when your estimated win probability for a team is higher than the probability implied by the sportsbook's odds. Use an Expected Value Calculator to verify.
