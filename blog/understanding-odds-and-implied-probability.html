Understanding Betting Odds and Implied Probability

By WagerAssistant Editorial Team · Published October 12, 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Odds are just probability expressed as a price — learn to read all three formats.
  • Implied probability = how likely the odds say an outcome is.
  • Books build in a margin (the vig); remove it to find true probability.
  • Value exists when your estimated probability beats the implied probability.

Every bet you place is really a disagreement about probability. The sportsbook sets a price; you decide whether that price is too high or too low. To bet profitably you must understand what odds actually represent and how to translate any price into a probability you can evaluate.

The three odds formats

American (moneyline) odds

American odds use positive and negative numbers. A negative number (-200) is the amount you must stake to win $100. A positive number (+180) is the profit on a $100 stake. The bigger the negative number, the stronger the favorite.

Decimal odds

Decimal odds show the total return per $1 staked, including your stake. At 3.00, a $10 bet returns $30 ($20 profit). They are the easiest format for calculating payouts: stake × decimal odds = total return.

Fractional odds

Common in the UK, fractional odds like 7/2 mean you win $7 for every $2 staked. They express profit relative to stake, so 7/2 is the same as 4.50 in decimal.

Converting between formats

Switching formats is essential when you shop lines across books that display odds differently. Rather than doing the arithmetic by hand, use the Odds Converter to move instantly between American, decimal and fractional prices. The underlying math: decimal = 1 + (profit/stake); an American favorite converts as decimal = 1 + (100/|odds|); an underdog as decimal = 1 + (odds/100).

What is implied probability?

Implied probability is the chance of an outcome that a given price suggests. It is the bridge between odds and value. The formula from decimal odds is simple: implied probability = 1 ÷ decimal odds. So odds of 2.00 imply a 50% chance; 4.00 implies 25%; 1.50 implies about 66.7%.

From American odds, a favorite's implied probability = |odds| ÷ (|odds| + 100), and an underdog's = 100 ÷ (odds + 100). Our Implied Probability Calculator handles all formats automatically.

Removing the vig

If you add up the implied probabilities of all outcomes in a market, they total more than 100%. That extra is the bookmaker's margin, known as the vig or juice — it is how the book profits. A typical two-way market might sum to 104–106%.

To estimate the true probability of an outcome, you need to remove this margin. The No-Vig Calculator and Margin Calculator strip out the juice so you can compare a book's genuine opinion against your own.

Finding value bets

A value bet exists when your estimate of an outcome's probability is higher than the implied probability baked into the odds. If you believe a team has a 55% chance to win but the price implies only 50%, that is a positive expected value (+EV) bet over the long run.

You will not win every value bet — variance guarantees losing streaks — but consistently betting into prices that underrate the true probability is the mathematical foundation of profitable betting. Quantify it with the Expected Value Calculator.

Mastering odds and implied probability turns betting from guesswork into a measurable decision. Once you can read any price as a probability and compare it to your own estimate, you are thinking like a sharp bettor.
