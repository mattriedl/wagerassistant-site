Craps Best Bets: The Lowest House Edge Wagers

Key takeaways

  • The pass line bet has a low 1.41% house edge.
  • Backing bets with free odds lowers your overall edge toward zero.
  • Don't pass/don't come offer a marginally lower edge at 1.36%.
  • Avoid proposition and hardway bets — some exceed a 16% house edge.

Craps looks chaotic, with its crowded table and dozens of betting options, but beneath the noise lies a game that offers some of the best odds in the casino — if you stick to the right bets. The difference between craps' best and worst wagers is enormous, so knowing which to make is the whole game.

How craps odds work

Craps revolves around the roll of two dice. A round begins with a 'come-out' roll. The most common bets follow the shooter's point number and resolve based on whether the shooter rolls the point again or sevens out. Because different bets have wildly different house edges, craps rewards players who understand the math and punishes those who chase the flashy center-table wagers.

Pass line and come bets

The pass line is the foundational craps bet and one of the best. You win on a come-out roll of 7 or 11, lose on 2, 3 or 12, and otherwise a 'point' is established that you then need to repeat before a 7. The house edge is just 1.41%.

The come bet works identically but is made after the point is established, effectively starting a fresh pass-line sequence for you. It carries the same low 1.41% edge, letting you have multiple numbers working at once with excellent odds.

Free odds: the best bet

Once a point is set, you can back your pass or come bet with an 'odds' bet — and this is the single best wager in the entire casino. The free odds bet pays at true odds with a 0% house edge. It does not replace your original bet; it supplements it.

Because the odds portion has no house edge, taking maximum odds dramatically lowers your combined house edge. With 2x odds, a pass line bet's effective edge drops to about 0.6%; with 5x odds it falls below 0.4%. Always take the maximum free odds your bankroll allows.

Don't pass and don't come

The 'don't' bets are the mirror image — you are betting with the house against the shooter. Don't pass and don't come carry a slightly lower house edge of 1.36%. You can also 'lay' odds behind them at zero house edge.

These bets win less often emotionally (you are rooting against the table), but mathematically they are marginally better than the pass line. Both are excellent low-edge choices; pick whichever suits your temperament.

Bets to avoid

The center of the table is where casinos make their money. These proposition and single-roll bets carry brutal house edges:

  • Any 7: around 16.7% house edge — the worst bet on the table.
  • Hardways: 9–11% depending on the number.
  • Field bets: often 5.5% despite looking attractive.
  • Big 6 / Big 8: 9% edge when the same number placed costs far less.

The rule is simple: stick to the pass/don't pass and come/don't come bets, always back them with free odds, and ignore the tempting center-table props. Do that and craps becomes one of the smartest games in the house. Quantify any wager's long-run cost with our Expected Value Calculator.
