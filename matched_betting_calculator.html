How Matched Betting Works

Matched betting uses bookmaker promotions (free bets, bonuses) to guarantee a profit by placing opposing bets:

  • Back bet: Bet on an outcome at the bookmaker
  • Lay bet: Bet against the same outcome on an exchange

Qualifying Bet: A normal bet to unlock a free bet offer. You accept a small loss (qualifying loss) to earn the free bet.

SNR (Stake Not Returned): The most common free bet type. If it wins, you get the profit but not the original stake back. Typical conversion: 60–80%.

SR (Stake Returned): If the free bet wins, you get both the profit and the stake. Higher conversion rate, typically 80–95%.

Tip: Look for odds between 3.0–6.0 for the best balance between qualifying loss and free bet conversion.

The Matched Betting Formula Explained

Matched betting pairs a back bet at a bookmaker with a lay bet at an exchange to extract the value from free bets risk-free. This matched betting calculator computes the exact lay stake and liability so your outcome is identical whether the selection wins or loses.

Formula & Worked Example

The lay stake is (Back Odds x Back Stake) / (Lay Odds - Commission). Back $10 at 3.0 while the exchange lays at 3.1 with 2% commission: lay stake = (3.0 x 10) / (3.1 - 0.02) = 30 / 3.08 = $9.74. The liability is 9.74 x (3.1 - 1) = $20.45. This near-perfect match means you lose only a few cents on a qualifying bet, then profit when you repeat with a free bet.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Ignoring exchange commission, which changes the correct lay stake.
  • Laying at odds far from the back price, creating a large qualifying loss.
  • Forgetting the lay liability must be covered in your exchange balance.
  • Mixing up stake-returned and stake-not-returned free bet modes.
