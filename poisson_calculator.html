How Poisson Distribution Works

The Poisson distribution models the probability of a given number of events in a fixed interval. For soccer, P(k goals) = (λ^k × e^−λ) / k! where λ is the expected goals. The probability of a specific scoreline (e.g., 2-1) is P(Home=2) × P(Away=1), assuming independence. This model is widely used for predicting soccer match outcomes.

The Poisson calculator uses the Poisson distribution to turn expected goals into scoreline probabilities for soccer matches. The Poisson Distribution Formula Explained

The Poisson distribution models how often a team scores given its expected goals. This poisson calculator turns an expected-goals average into the probability of each exact scoreline, which you can combine into match-result and over/under prices.

Formula & Worked Example

The formula is P(k) = (lambda^k x e^-lambda) / k!, where lambda is expected goals and k is the number scored. With lambda = 1.4, the chance of exactly 2 goals is (1.4^2 x e^-1.4) / 2! = (1.96 x 0.2466) / 2 = 0.242, or 24.2%. Doing this for both teams and multiplying scoreline probabilities builds the full match grid.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using season-long averages instead of opponent-adjusted expected goals.
  • Assuming goals are fully independent - correlation exists in real matches.
  • Ignoring that Poisson slightly underrates draws and low-scoring games.
  • Feeding in a lambda that already bakes in home advantage twice.
