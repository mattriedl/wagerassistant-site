The Kelly Criterion Formula

f = (bp − q) / b where: f = fraction of bankroll to bet, b = decimal odds − 1, p = probability of winning, q = probability of losing (1 − p). If f is negative, the bet has negative expected value and should be avoided. Half/Quarter Kelly reduces volatility at the cost of slower growth.

Why Use Our Kelly Criterion Calculator?

Bet sizing is just as important as bet selection. Staking too much risks ruin; too little leaves growth on the table. The Kelly Criterion finds the balance, and using Half or Quarter Kelly reduces variance for a smoother bankroll curve.

Pro Tips for Smarter Betting

  • Most professionals use Half or Quarter Kelly to reduce swings.
  • Kelly only recommends a bet when you have a positive edge — a negative result means no bet.
  • Your edge is only as good as your probability estimate; be honest and conservative.

The Kelly Criterion Formula Explained

The Kelly Criterion sizes each bet as a fraction of your bankroll based on your edge. This kelly criterion calculator turns your estimated win probability and the offered odds into an optimal stake that maximizes long-term growth while avoiding ruin.

Formula & Worked Example

The formula is f = (bp - q) / b, where b is decimal odds minus 1, p is your win probability and q is 1 - p. At decimal 2.50 (b = 1.5) with p = 0.45, the math is (1.5 x 0.45 - 0.55) / 1.5 = 0.0833, or 8.33% of your bankroll - about $83 of a $1000 roll. Most pros use half-Kelly, which here would stake $41.50 to cut variance.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Overestimating your win probability, which tells Kelly to bet far too much.
  • Using full Kelly, whose swings are brutal - fractional Kelly is safer.
  • Betting Kelly on negative-edge wagers, where the formula returns zero or less.
  • Recalculating off the original bankroll instead of the current one.
