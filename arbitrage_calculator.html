How Arbitrage Betting Works

Arbitrage occurs when the combined implied probability of all outcomes is less than 100%. This means you can bet on every outcome and guarantee a profit. Arb Margin = (1/Dec1 + 1/Dec2) — if below 1.0, an arb exists. The calculator automatically computes the optimal stake on Outcome 2 to equalize profit.

The arbitrage calculator finds ‘sure bets’ — situations where differing prices across sportsbooks let you back every outcome and lock in a guaranteed profit. Enter the two opposing prices and your stake, and this sure bet calculator returns the exact stake for each side, your total investment, ROI and the arbitrage margin.

Arbitrage betting is risk-free in theory because you profit regardless of the result. Our sports arbitrage calculator does the precise stake-balancing math for you so the profit is split evenly across outcomes.

Pro Tips for Smarter Betting

  • An arb exists only when the combined implied probability is under 100%.
  • Act fast — lines move quickly and books may limit accounts that arb heavily.
  • Account for commission, currency and stake limits before committing.

The Arbitrage Betting Formula Explained

Arbitrage (or a surebet) exists when the combined implied probability of all outcomes across different books is below 100%. This arbitrage betting calculator checks for that gap and splits your stake so every result returns the same profit.

Formula & Worked Example

The test is (1 / OddsA) + (1 / OddsB) < 1. Say Book A offers +110 (2.10) on one side and Book B offers -105 (1.952) on the other. Then 1/2.10 + 1/1.952 = 0.4762 + 0.5123 = 0.9885, a 1.16% arb. Splitting a $1000 bankroll in proportion gives $481.74 on side A and $518.26 on side B, locking in roughly $11.60 profit no matter who wins.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Bets being voided or limited before you place both legs, leaving you exposed.
  • Odds moving between placing the first and second leg.
  • Ignoring commissions or currency fees that erase a thin arb.
  • Rounding stakes so the profit is no longer equal across outcomes.
