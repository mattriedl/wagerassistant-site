How No-Vig Odds Work

Bookmakers add a margin (vig/juice) to their odds so the implied probabilities total more than 100%. The no-vig calculator removes this margin proportionally: Fair Probability = Implied Probability / Total Implied Probability. Fair Odds = 1 / Fair Probability. This reveals the bookmaker's true assessment of each outcome without their profit margin.

The no-vig calculator removes the bookmaker's built-in margin (the vig or juice) to reveal the true probabilities and fair odds behind a market. Enter the prices for each outcome and this true odds calculator strips out the overround so you can see what the line would be in a fair, margin-free market.

Comparing a sportsbook's price to its no-vig fair price is one of the sharpest ways to find value. This remove vig calculator turns posted prices into the unbiased probabilities you need for +EV analysis.

Why Use Our No-Vig Calculator?

Every posted price includes a margin that hides the true probability. By removing the vig, you get a cleaner estimate of an outcome's real chance — invaluable for comparing books, building models and identifying when another sportsbook is offering a +EV price.

Pro Tips for Smarter Betting

  • Use the sharpest book's no-vig line as a benchmark for fair value.
  • If another book's price beats the fair odds, you may have found a +EV bet.
  • Two-way markets are simplest; the same method extends to three-way markets.

The No-Vig Fair Odds Formula Explained

Sportsbooks pad every market with vig, so the quoted odds overstate the true edge. This no vig calculator strips out that margin and reveals the fair probability and fair odds each outcome deserves.

Formula & Worked Example

Fair probability is (1/your odds) / (sum of 1/all odds). On a -110 / -110 market, each side's raw implied probability is 52.38%, summing to 104.76%. Dividing gives 52.38 / 104.76 = 50%, so the fair odds are exactly 2.00 on both sides. Comparing a book's actual price to this no-vig number is how sharps spot genuine value.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Applying the method to a book with lopsided, inaccurate prices.
  • Assuming no-vig odds are a guaranteed prediction rather than a fair baseline.
  • Forgetting to include every outcome (e.g. the draw in soccer) in the sum.
  • Comparing no-vig odds from a soft book instead of a sharp one.
